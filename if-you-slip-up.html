When things go sideways

If you slip up

Healing is not a straight line, and this court knows it. What you do next matters more than the slip itself.

Missed a court date? Call your lawyer or First Nations Court duty counsel right away: 604-601-6074 in Greater Vancouver, or 1-877-601-6066 anywhere else in BC.

Tell someone first

If you've missed an appointment or broken a condition, don't sit on it. Tell your courtworker, your lawyer, or duty counsel as soon as you can, before your next court date if possible.

They've seen this before. They can help you figure out the next step, and it's much easier for them to help when they hear it from you early.

You can reach a Native Courtworker toll free at 1-877-811-1190.

What can happen

The conditions in your healing plan usually live inside a court order: bail, probation, or a conditional sentence order.

Breaking a court order is serious. It can be a new criminal charge on its own, and the penalties can include jail. Legal Aid BC puts it plainly: judges view a breach of a court order as a serious matter.

That's the honest picture. It's also why speaking up early beats staying quiet.

What the court expects

Being willing to follow the sentence and the healing plan is part of taking part in this court. You said yes to the work when you chose it.

The Elders and the judge are on your side, and they hold you accountable too. Don't assume this court means a lighter or easier sentence.

Don't disappear

Disappearing is the worst response. Asking for help early is the best one.

A hard stretch doesn't erase your progress. Review hearings exist so the judge and Elders can check on you and support you, through exactly this kind of moment.
