# Author's firsthand observations

These are the author's own observations from attending New Westminster First Nations Court with a client who was making a first appearance, an introduction to the court. They may be used as source material for the site, especially the court day page. Practice varies by location and by day, so pages drawing on these notes must present them as what the author saw at New Westminster, using "may" and "often" wording.

- New Westminster First Nations Court sometimes sits at the Inn at the Quay hotel in New Westminster and sometimes at the Law Courts in New Westminster. The process is similar at both, but the Inn can feel less intimidating. The room there looks out over the water. (The court's own schedule confirms the two venues: it marks sittings "Hyack" for the Inn at the Quay's Hyack Room and "NWLC" for the Law Courts.)
- At the Inn, one of the Elders prepares smudging outside by the water and smudges people one by one with an eagle feather, before court.
- Everyone sits in a circle. Introductions go around the circle counterclockwise, starting with the judge.
- Present in the circle: the judge, Elders, duty counsel, Crown counsel, Native Courtworkers, the person appearing, and their supporters.
- Native Courtworkers come and talk with the client, build a connection, and help with practical needs like housing.

Note: no official source mentions eagle feathers or the counterclockwise order at BC's Indigenous sentencing courts. These details are attributed to the author's observation only.
